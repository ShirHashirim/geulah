## 1998: The Jubilee of Bones and Forces

In the Hebrew year 5758, Israel reached its **50th anniversary**. In biblical tradition, the Jubilee represents a return to one’s ancestral holding and a restoration of the land. It was a moment where the physical body of the nation appeared complete—the **"gathering of the bones"** was visible in the thriving cities, the military might, and the settled hills. Yet, beneath this physical strength, the "spirit" had not yet entered, leaving the nation to rely on **human forces** rather than **enduring purpose**.

### 1. The Reliance on "The Forces"
The political spectrum in 1998 was defined by an attempt to secure the land through tactical positioning rather than spiritual consensus. 
*   **Tactical Shielding:** Leadership sought to protect the nation’s territorial integrity by bringing in "strongmen" to shore up political flanks. The logic was one of **force and manpower**—using the reputation of hawks to buy time for diplomatic maneuvers.
*   **The Land as a Bargaining Chip:** Because the trust was placed in the "forces" of political survival, the land itself began to feel "for sale." Concessions were weighed in the scales of coalition math, leading to a profound sense of unease among those who viewed the restoration as a divine, non-negotiable decree.

### 2. The Liberal Secular Defensiveness
While some focused on the land, the liberal secular agenda was fighting its own "war of attrition" over the soul of the state:
*   **Culture War:** The Jubilee celebrations became a battleground for the public square. Without a unified spirit, the 50th year highlighted a fractured identity, where creative freedom and traditional values clashed.
*   **Domestic Self-Defense:** The liberal camp shifted from universal hopes to "local autonomy," seeking to protect their secular way of life through legal and social barriers, further dividing the "body" of the nation into separate enclaves.

### 3. The Fragility of the Threshold
By the end of 1998, the reliance on "forces" proved to be a house of cards. 
*   **The Breakdown:** The very alliances formed to protect the status quo collapsed under the weight of their own contradictions. The government fell not because of an external enemy, but because the "bones" could not hold together without a shared internal breath.
*   **The Great Paradox:** This year set the stage for the ultimate irony: the same political forces trusted to guard the settlements would, within a few short years, use that same executive power to dismantle them. This revealed that a nation relying on **political force** without **spiritual permanence** is always subject to the shifting winds of leadership.
